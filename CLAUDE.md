# FlipUp: notes for Claude

Poker night on your phones: no-limit Texas Hold'em for play chips. A host opens a table and friends join on their own phones with a 5-letter code; bots fill empty seats. There's also a bots-only mode that runs entirely on one phone. Read `docs/RULES.md` before changing any poker rule.

## Commands

- `npm run dev`: app plus the Worker and Durable Object, all local (http://localhost:5173)
- `npm run typecheck`: `tsc -b` over app, worker and tooling projects
- `npm test`: Vitest unit tests for `src/game`
- `npm run e2e`: Playwright, phone-sized Chromium, starts its own dev server on 5174. In the cloud sandbox Chromium is at `/opt/pw-browsers` and `@playwright/test` is pinned to 1.56.1 to match it. `SHOTS_DIR=/some/dir` saves a screenshot of each screen.
- `npm run check`: typecheck + unit tests + build. Run it before committing.
- `npm run deploy`: build and `wrangler deploy` (needs `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` and `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID` in the environment; never commit them).
- After editing `wrangler.jsonc`, run `npm run cf-typegen` and commit `worker-configuration.d.ts`.

## Layout

- `src/game/`: the pure TypeScript poker engine, shared by the app and the server. No DOM or Node APIs here.
  - `cards.ts`, `evaluate.ts` (hand ranking), `equity.ts` (odds and the bots' hand strength)
  - `holdem.ts`: dealing, blinds, betting rounds, side pots, showdown
  - `reducer.ts`: `applyAction()`, the only way a table changes. Server and phone run the same code.
  - `bots.ts`, `selectors.ts`, `validate.ts` (`parseAction()` for anything from the network), `redact.ts`
  - Bots: 40 of them (`botStyles.ts`), four each of ten hidden styles. `botPlay.ts` decides: preflop from GTO charts (`botCharts.json`, 2–6 players at 100/40/20bb; 7–9 handed fold into the 6-max chart) bent by style, push/fold when short, and an equity check against the raiser's range for big raises and all-ins; postflop it imagines the opponents' hands from their public story (`botRange.ts`: preflop line plus every postflop move, weighted by how well each hand fits, adjusted by `game.reads`), then weighs check/bet sizes/call/raise/fold by equity, fold equity and what it wins when called, blended with a slice of GTO's mix from `botPostflop.json`. Styles set how much a bot reads ranges (`sense`), its leaks, bet-size tells, tempo and when it shows cards. `botEval.ts` is a fast evaluator (matches `scoreHand`) so a decision stays within a couple of ms on the server. `botMind.ts` keeps each bot's tilt, confidence and grudges in `game.minds`. `botTalk.ts` holds their fixed comment lines (ids `b:...`, never accepted from phones). A bot that speaks before the outcome ("c'mon!", a shove) is in `game.sweating` and always reacts when the hand ends (`afterHand()` in `reducer.ts`, `owed` skips the cooldown and the dice). On the phone, `useReactions()` in `src/screens/table/Bubbles.tsx` shows bot lines one at a time, holds outcome reactions back half a second to a second, and drops "c'mon" lines when the next card lands or the pot goes. `redactForDevice()` strips styles, reads, minds and plans. Rebuild the data with `gto/scripts/export_bot_charts.py` and `gto/scripts/train_bots.py` (its `--parity` output is `botPlay.parity.json`, which the unit test checks the TypeScript buckets against). `botSim.ts` plays bot tables (and scripted players, including a constant bluffer, `bluffBot`) for tuning; the bots note how often a player bets (`betFreq`) and call lighter against a constant bettor, and punish players who fold too much (`EXPLOIT` in `botPlay.ts`; see `docs/BOTS.md`); `scripts/botLab.ts` runs the full lab and fun score (see `docs/BOTS.md`), with the bots from before the update in `scripts/legacy/` to compare against.
- Home (`src/screens/Home.tsx`, `HomeLearn.tsx`, `HomeReview.tsx`, `src/styles/home.css`): no wordmark; a large title with the streak pill and your avatar beside it (the avatar opens your profile, where Settings lives), a banner card only when there's one clear next step (your open table, the online table you left, or your last game's unreviewed hands), grouped rows with coloured icon squares, a shelf of at most five quick bot games (`HomeQuick.tsx`, titled by the bot's name) and a floating tab bar: Play, Learn (solved hands, Poker library, Custom hand) and Review (the coach's next step, your GTO match, Review hands, Your stats, Insights, Study bank), with a round "Join with a code" button beside it (a left rail on desktop). The daily streak counts days with a hand played (`flipup.days`). Home never shows chip totals: stacks reset every game, so progress is the GTO match, and Insights (`HomeInsights.tsx`, per-table results in `flipup.stats.games`) counts tables by where you finished (`place`). The coach types its Home greeting once per visit.
- Scroll, shown as "Solved hands" on the Learn tab (`src/screens/train/SolvedHands.tsx`, `src/quiz/`, banner in `HomeLearn.tsx`, data in `public/gto/quiz`): whole multiway hands the GTO solver played out ahead of time (`gto/scripts/quiz_multiway.py` with the multiway solver, packed by `gto/scripts/export_quiz.py`): solved 6-max preflop with nobody forced to fold, and only hands where three or four players see the flop; no bot styles, no heads-up hands. The hand plays on the replay table and stops at every one of your decisions with every legal option; your pick is graded at once against the solver's mix for your exact cards, and the coach says why (`readSpot()` in `quiz/read.ts` reads the hand, board, position, price and the multiway picture: players in, callers in front, players behind, each defender's share of the defence; the wording is `quizWhy()` in `train/coach.ts`). Then the hand carries on the solved line. No server. Study bank (`StudyBank.tsx`, a row on the Review tab): Save names a hand and keeps it in `flipup.quizbank` until deleted; every finished hand sits in `flipup.quizrecent` for 24 hours (`loadRecent()` deletes older ones), and Play again opens it by id (`{ name: 'scroll', hand }`). The old bot-style Scroll cards are gone (their saves, `flipup.scroll` and `flipup.scrollhands`, are cleared on load by `forgetOldScroll()`); Scroll never mentions play styles or exploits.
- GTO Coach (`src/train/Coach.tsx`, wording in `src/train/coach.ts`): a 🧑‍💻 avatar named "GTO Coach" that gives all advice in speech bubbles, laid out like Messages: the face on the left, the name as a small caption, grey bubbles in regular weight (only a graded move tints them), and a button inside the stack whenever the coach asks a question (reviews, replay, drills, stats, the Review tab). New advice copy goes in `coach.ts`: short, plain, contractions, no jargon until "Show me the numbers". Grades read Good / Close call / Costly.
- Replay (`src/screens/train/Replay.tsx`, `src/train/replay.ts`): watch a saved hand back from its `HandRecord` (works for online tables too, since the record only holds the cards that phone could see). `buildFrames()` is pure; the screen has no action buttons and never shows hidden cards. Opens from Insights, the review list, a review and the table between hands.
- `src/gto/preflop.ts`: preflop drills dealt on the phone from `public/gto/preflop-6p-100bb.json` (a solved 6-handed, 100bb game). `getDrill()` in `src/gto/api.ts` tries it first for the six preflop scenarios and falls back to the coach service. Rebuild the file with `gto/scripts/export_preflop.py` (needs the built C++ engine, see `gto/README.md`); `preflop.reference.json` holds solver numbers the unit test checks the file against, so regenerate both together.
- Join popup (`src/screens/sheets/JoinSheet.tsx`, scanner in `src/ui/QRScanner.tsx`): "Join with a code" opens a sheet with five code boxes (the keyboard only opens when you tap them) and a "Scan a QR code" button. The scanner uses `BarcodeDetector` where the browser has it and `jsqr` otherwise. A join link (`/ABCDE`) still opens the small `Join` screen in `Join.tsx`.
- The table screen: `src/screens/Table.tsx` is the frame (header, felt, timer, win banner); `table/Pods.tsx` is the other players, your box and the "+N" list, `table/Bubbles.tsx` is speech bubbles and quick messages, `table/Actions.tsx` is the action row, pre-actions and the raise panel.
- `src/state/store.ts`: the app store and every screen command. Bots-only tables run `applyAction()` locally with their own clock; online tables send actions to the server and show what it sends back.
- `src/ui/sound.ts` (synthesised table sounds, no files) and `src/ui/useTableSounds.ts` (plays them by watching how the game changes). `Count` in `components.tsx` is the counting-number animation for pot and stacks.
- Per-phone settings live in `src/state/persist.ts`: table felt/rail (`flipup.table`), Appearance (`flipup.appearance`: auto follows the phone, or light, or dark), Transparency (`flipup.glass`), mute, lifetime totals and the last online table for Home. Settings (Home → your avatar → Settings, and the "You" part of the table menu) is one grouped list with iPhone switches (`Rows` in `SheetHost.tsx`). Haptics (`haptic()` in `src/ui/effects.ts`) use `navigator.vibrate` on Android and, on iPhone, flip a hidden switch-style checkbox, which Safari 18+ answers with a Taptic tick; buttons show a press by shrinking and dimming (no ripples). Pre-actions (Check/Fold, Call current, Call any) are screen state in `Table.tsx`, never sent to the server until your turn. Off your turn they fill the same three slots as Fold, Check and Raise; Call current clears itself if the bet changes.
- Desktop: `src/styles/desktop.css` is one media query (min-width 960px and min-height 600px, the same as `useDesktop` in `src/ui/useDesktop.ts`), so phones never see it. `installDesktopScale()` sets `--desk-zoom` from a 1440×900 baseline; the table is `zoom`ed by it. On desktop the table has a `.stage` (players round the oval, positions from `--ax`/`--ay` set in `Table.tsx`) and a side panel (`SidePanel.tsx`: standings and live hand log). On a phone `.stage` is `display: contents` and the row of four slides. Keep phone CSS outside that media query.
- A new game waits `TIMING.startDelay` (3s) after Deal before the first hand (`start` in `reducer.ts` sets `nextHandAt`; the table shows "Dealing in 3…"). Inside it a bot greets through `pendingTalk`: `hello` heads-up, naming the person (lines hold `{you}`, `reactionText(id, name)` fills it, and `reactions[id].to` says who), `welcome` for a bigger table.
- With more than four others the row is a window; the "+N" badge (`HiddenPlayers` in `table/Pods.tsx`) opens a small list of the players out of it with their chips.
- Play again (`playAgain` action, `openAgain()` in `reducer.ts`, buttons in `Final.tsx`): on a finished table the host's tap turns it into a new lobby (new `game.id`, the blinds and bot count from `game.first`, fresh stacks); a friend's tap only adds them to `game.again`, and only those people are seated when the host opens it. Anyone not seated sees "A new game is open" with a Sit down button (`Lobby.tsx`). Names are unique per table: a taken one gets " 2", " 3"… (`uniqueName()`), within the 10 character limit. `useWakeLock()` keeps the screen on while a table is going.
- Offline start: `public/sw.js` (registered in `src/main.tsx`, production only) keeps the app so Home, Insights, Train and bot games open with no connection. Pages are network first (a new deploy arrives at once), files under `/assets`, `/sounds`, `/icons` and `/gto` are cached on first use, `/api` is never touched, and an HTML answer is never stored as a script (the single-page fallback returns one for missing files). Bump `VERSION` in it if its behaviour changes. `e2e/offline.spec.ts` builds the app and runs it with `vite preview`.
- A dropped phone: the table room (`worker/room.ts`) tells the game which devices are connected with the internal `presence` action (never accepted from a phone, like `tick`). A person who isn't connected gets `player.offline`, shown as an "Offline" tag on their seat, and when the table has a clock their turn is capped at `TIMING.offlineClock` (15s). A table with no clock is left alone.
- The hand log's share button (`handLogText()` in `src/game/handText.ts`) sends a finished hand as plain text through the phone's share sheet, or copies it.
- Quick messages are `react` actions from the fixed list in `src/game/reactions.ts`; nothing typed is ever accepted.
- AI twin memory (`src/game/twin.ts`, `src/state/twin.ts`, plan in `docs/TWINS.md`): what a twin knows about a player. Pure counters built from each finished `HandRecord` and the player's own hole cards: how often they fold, check, call, bet small or big, raise or move in per spot (street, position, hand strength, what they face, players in), plus VPIP, PFR, 3-bet, c-bet, fold-to-bet, bluff share and bet sizes by hand strength. `captureFinishedHand()` feeds it, so every hand you play counts, bots and online included. It lives in `flipup.twin`, apart from the 24 hour hand history, and Settings has "Reset my AI twin". Ready to play from `TWIN_READY` (50) hands, full confidence at 300. `fitStyle()` in `twinFit.ts` turns it into a bot `Style` (the ten styles blended by how close their numbers are, per `twinRef.json`, then nudged by the gains in `FIT`) and `buildBrain()` adds the spot counts: that `TwinBrain` sits on `Player.twin`, which `redactForDevice()` strips. `decide()` in `botPlay.ts` plays the seat's `playerStyle()` and asks `twinLook()` (`twinMix.ts`) what the person did in spots like this one (same street, a hand about as strong, facing about the same bet, weighted by how alike they are); the bot's odds are pulled towards that by `n / (n + 15)`. The bots' own "never fold the nuts" guards run before the blend, so a twin makes its person's mistakes. `scripts/twinLab.ts` (`ref`, `fit`, `tune`) measures the ten styles into `twinRef.json` and checks a twin against the player it came from; rerun `ref` if the bots change. On Play, `HomeTwins.tsx` shows the AI twins tiles (a face looking into a mirror, `MirrorArt` in `CardArt.tsx`): "Your twin" deals a heads-up game with `startTwin()` once the memory has `TWIN_READY` hands (before that it opens `TwinSheet.tsx`), and "Friends' twins" says "Coming soon". The twin seat has `Player.ai` (public, shown as an "AI" chip) and the table `Game.twinGame` (so Play again calls `startTwin()` again). Hand and board features (`handBucket()`, `boardTexture()`) live in `handBucket.ts`, re-exported by `botPlay.ts`.
- `worker/room.ts`: one `TableRoom` Durable Object per join code, the only true copy of an online table and its deck. Its alarm is the table's clock (bots, turn timers, run-outs, next deal) and also deletes tables idle for 3 days.

## Rules that matter

- Hidden information stays hidden. `redactForDevice()` strips the deck and every hole card that isn't yours or face up. Bots-only tables keep the deck on the phone but the screens still only get the redacted view.
- Win/tie odds show only during an all-in run-out, when every live hand is face up. Never while betting is still possible (product owner's call).
- Every timed thing goes through `game.wakeAt` and a `tick` action. `parseAction()` never accepts `tick` from a phone.
- Chips are never created or lost: the "keeps every chip accounted for" test plays bot hands and checks the total after every step. Keep it passing.
- When a poker rule is unclear, ask the product owner, then write the answer in `docs/RULES.md`.

## UI style

Apple-like: it should feel like Apple made a poker app. System font only (SF Pro on iPhone, the platform default elsewhere), regular weight for body text, semibold for headings and buttons, bold only for large titles. Light mode is white with `#f2f2f7` cells, dark mode is true black with `#1c1c1e` cells, and the accent is system blue. Cells are flat (a soft fill, no outline, no shadow). Glass (the blurred, see-through look) stays on what floats: the tab bar, capsules, sheets and toasts. Large titles on Home, grouped rows with a coloured icon square and a chevron, a banner card with its own action bar for the one main next step. Instant feedback (press scale, haptics, confetti, card flips) that never blocks play. Profile pictures are emojis (pick from the grid or type any). Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Plain writing: no buzzwords, contractions are fine.

Every colour, size, radius and shadow is a token in `:root` in `src/styles/global.css`. Colours needed from code live in `src/ui/theme.ts`. Don't hard-code new values; spacing is on a 4px grid.
