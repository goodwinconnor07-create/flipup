# PART 1: POKER IN 60 SECONDS (so you can show it correctly)

FlipUp is built on **Texas Hold'em**, the most popular form of poker.

- **Hole cards.** Each player gets **two private cards** face down. Only they can see them.
- **The board.** Up to **five shared cards** come face up in the middle of the table, in three steps: **the flop** (three cards at once), **the turn** (a fourth card) and **the river** (a fifth card).
- **Betting rounds.** Before the flop and after each new card, players take turns. They can **fold** (give up the hand), **check** (pass when there's nothing to match), **call** (match the current bet), **bet** or **raise** (put in more), or go **all in** (bet every chip they have).
- **The pot.** All the chips bet in a hand go into the pot in the middle.
- **The showdown.** If two or more players are still in after the river, they turn their cards over. The best **five-card hand** made from your two cards plus the five on the board wins the pot. From best to worst: royal flush, straight flush, four of a kind, full house, flush, straight, three of a kind, two pair, one pair, high card.
- **Positions.** Players sit in a circle. The **dealer button (BTN)** moves one seat each hand. The two players after it post forced bets called the **small blind (SB)** and **big blind (BB)**. Other seats have names too, like **UTG** ("under the gun", first to act), **HJ** (hijack) and **CO** (cutoff). Acting later is an advantage.
- **Bluffing.** You can bet with a weak hand to make others fold. That's what makes poker a game of reading people.
- **A "range".** Good players never put an opponent on one exact hand. They think about **all the hands that opponent could have**, given how they've played. That group of possible hands is their **range**. Working out an opponent's range and choosing the play that beats it is **the single most important skill in poker**, and it's what FlipUp's Range Trainer teaches.

# PART 2: FLIPUP FROM SCRATCH

## 2.1 What FlipUp is, in one breath

FlipUp is **poker night on your phone**. You can play Texas Hold'em and **12 other original poker games** with your friends, each on their own phone, by sharing a short code. You can play against **40 bot characters** who each have their own personality, playing style and voice. You can play a whole **tournament of different games** with friends in **Party mode**. And you can **train**: the **Range Trainer** puts you in real poker spots and teaches you to answer the only two questions that matter, *"What is my opponent's range? And what do I want to do about it?"*

Three pillars: **Play. Train. Bots.** Plus Premium, which unlocks everything.

## 2.2 The brand and the look (copy this style exactly)

- **Name:** FlipUp. (The live web address is gtoduel.online; the brand in the ad is always **FlipUp**.)
- **Design language:** Apple-like. It should look like Apple designed it.
  - **Font:** the system font only (SF Pro on iPhone). Regular weight for body text, **semibold** for headings and buttons, **bold** only for huge titles.
  - **Light mode:** white pages with soft light-grey cells (#f2f2f7). **Dark mode:** true black pages with dark grey cells (#1c1c1e).
  - **Cells are flat:** a soft fill with no outline and no drop shadow.

## 2.10 THE RANGE TRAINER (the hero feature: explain it in full)

### What it is
A library of **219 hand-built poker spots**, each one **theory-approved, pro-approved and checked by the maths**. In every spot, the correct answer has to beat the other options by a clear margin in the app's own equity and expected-value numbers. These aren't random quizzes: each is a real, well-known kind of situation that decides whether players win or lose, like a bluff catch on the river, a thin value bet, folding a strong hand to a huge bet, or an overbet bluff.

Each spot teaches you to answer **two questions on every street:**
1. **"What is my opponent's range?"** What hands could they have, given everything they've done?
2. **"What do I want to do about it?"** Given that range, what's the best play?

That's the skill that wins at real tables, and it's exactly what FlipUp drills.

### The Train tab (what it looks like)
- **Always dark**, on a slowly breathing field of blue light: calm and focused, like a training room. **It reacts to you:** a best play warms the whole room with a **gold glow from below**, a good play warms it a little, a miss cools it to blue, and when today's quiz is done it settles into a still, calm gold glow.
- **Today's hand** is a wide card at the top: one new hand every day, **free, the same for everyone**, with **Play** and your **🔥 streak** beside it. Once played it shows your grade, a countdown to tomorrow's hand, **Your answer** and **Share**, plus an optional daily reminder.
- **"Keep training": three big picture tiles in a row**, drawn in the same sticker style as the games: **Endless** (hand after hand, with your run of best plays counting up), **Sets** (five hands, scored) and **Gaps** (a set built from your weakest spots and your misses). Without Premium they sit under a soft blurred lock: **"Unlock Premium to train"**. Below: the spots worth working on, your numbers and how much of the library you've mastered. Training works **offline** too.

### Playing a hand (the full flow; show all of this)
A set plays full screen. The top bar has **Back** and **five progress dots**.

1. **The spot.** A title (for example **"Ace high facing a pot-size river lead"**), a topic tag (for example **"Reading bet sizes"**), and the setting: *"You're BTN · 6-handed cash"* and the opponent type, for example *"BB · typical live player"*.
2. **The table:** a clean felt with every seat round it, labelled by position (no made-up faces), your seat at the bottom with your two cards (**A♥ 7♥**), the board (**J♠ 9♠ 2♣ 4♦ 2♦**), the pot (**"Pot 40.5bb"**) and the stacks (**"You have 190bb behind"**).
3. **The hand history**, written out like a story, one street per line: *"Preflop: UTG folds · HJ folds · CO folds · You raise to 5bb · SB folds · BB calls."* Then *"Flop J♠9♠2♣, pot 10.5bb: BB checks · You bet 5bb · BB calls."* Then *"Turn 4♦: BB checks · You check."* Then *"River 2♦: BB bets 20bb."* A small **Replay** button steps through the hand on the table if you want to watch it.
4. **The decision:** *"Your move: fold, call or raise?"* with big buttons along the bottom: **Fold · Call 20bb · Raise to 60bb**. (When nobody has bet, the choices are **Check · Bet ⅓ pot · Bet ¾ pot · Overbet**.)

# PART 7: ANIMATION, EDITING, SOUND AND TEXT

## 7.1 House motion style
Apple-like: springy but restrained. Entries ease out over about 250–280ms, starting from 95% size and transparent (never from nothing). Taps press down to 98%. No overshoot or bounce for its own sake. Reduce clutter: one thing moves at a time.

## 7.2 Signature moves (use throughout)
1. **The card-flip transition.** Scene changes happen *through* a card flipping: a card back fills the frame, it flips in 3D, and the next scene is on its face.
2. **Chip-slide wipes.** A neat stack of chips slides across the frame, revealing the next shot behind it.
3. **Tap ripples.** Every tap shows a soft circular ripple plus the button's press-down, so the viewer always knows what was pressed.
4. **Counting numbers.** Pots, stacks, percentages and the "219 spots" counter all count. The app does this itself; capture it rather than faking it.
5. **Pop-in labels.** Short labels (4 words max) pop beside the element they describe, joined by a thin line, then dissolve.
6. **Zoom punches.** A 1.15× push into the exact element on a beat (a range group pill, the code tiles, the power banner), then back out. Use sparingly: 6–8 times in the whole ad.
7. **Light sweeps.** A diagonal gloss sweeps across premium things: gold names, card backs, Premium tiles.
8. **Felt colour bleeds.** Between games, the next game's felt colour spreads out radially from the centre of the board.
9. **Speech bubble conveyor.** Bot lines appear as typing dots that turn into words arriving a few at a time, with a soft blip on each.
10. **Earned confetti.** Only for a correct training answer, a pot won and the party champion.
11. **Room light.** In the Range Trainer, the whole background warms gold for a best play and cools blue for a miss. Mirror this with the music (a warm chord, a cool filtered note).
12. **The range motif.** Whenever an opponent is on screen, faint group pills ("Missed draws", "Top pair") float around their emoji face like thought bubbles, tying the whole ad to the core idea.

## 7.3 Pacing
Cut on actions, not pauses. The average shot is 1.2–2 seconds, except in the Range Trainer explanation (3–4 seconds per beat, so people can read). Never hold a static screen for more than 2.5 seconds without motion. Prepare a **reduced-motion variant** with crossfades instead of whips.

## 7.4 Sound and music
- **Music:** modern, confident, minimal electronic or hip-hop, 110–120 BPM, with a strong drop right after the hook. Filter it down to soft pads in the Range Trainer, build back up through the GTO Wizard comparison, and hit full energy for Premium and the end card.
- **Sound design:** use the app's own sounds (it synthesises them: card flips, chip slides, the dice tumble, the ghost's "spook", the Power Cards "pop", a splash for Red River, ticks in the last five seconds of a turn). Add a soft tick per code letter, a whoosh on whip pans, a deep satisfying "thunk" when a verdict lands, and a fizzle for the robot short-circuit.
- **Voiceover:** warm, confident and slightly playful, like a friend who's very good at poker. Not a hype announcer. Contractions, short sentences.
- **Mix:** the VO always sits on top; duck the music about 6dB under it.

## 7.5 On-screen text rules
- Max 6 words per line, max 2 lines on screen at once.
- System font only. Semibold for captions, bold for the big title moments. White on dark scenes, black on light ones.
- Use the app's real colours: Apple blue for actions, gold for Premium and wins, green and red for range groups (beat and beaten), and each game's own colour while that game is on screen.
- Emoji only where the app uses them: game emoji, bot faces, 🔥 streak, ⚡, ⭐.
